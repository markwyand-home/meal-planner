# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

A two-person household — Mark and Erin — who plan and cook their own dinners. They
are both the planners and the eaters; there are no other roles, no admins, and no
outside audience. The weekly plan reaches them by email (recipients are listed in
`~/.meal-planner/recipients.txt`, outside this public repo) and as a hosted dashboard
they open on whatever device is at hand, including a phone at the grocery store.

## Product Purpose

Eliminate the recurring manual work of deciding what to eat, shopping for it, and
compiling a grocery list. Every Sunday morning the pipeline picks four dinners from
the household's own saved recipes, derives a consolidated grocery list, pushes it to
their AnyList shopping list, regenerates a dashboard of the week, and emails the
plan.

Success is a week that requires no decisions: the plan is in the inbox, the
groceries are already on the phone in AnyList, and the recipe pages are one tap
away. A partial run — a plan without a grocery list, a stale dashboard, a failed
push — is a failure, not a partial success, and is reported as such.

## Positioning

Not a recommendation engine and not a recipe discovery app. The universe of meals is
strictly the household's own vetted collection in `Recipes/`; nothing enters rotation
without being explicitly added. What the product contributes is the sequencing and
the plumbing — variety enforcement, consolidation, and delivery — over a corpus the
household already trusts. A generic meal-planning app cannot make that claim because
it does not start from a closed, hand-curated set.

Variety is the core selection value, on two axes at once: week to week (no repeats
within two weeks, recency down-weighting) and within a week (all four dinners use
different primary proteins — never three tofu dinners in a row).

## Operating Context

- Runs as a Claude scheduled task Sunday mornings (~6:47am), or on demand ("run the
  meal plan"). Scheduled runs start with no memory of prior conversation, so every
  instruction they need must live in the repo.
- The task only runs while the Claude desktop app is open; if closed, it runs at next
  launch. The emailing step needs a mail tool connected in that session.
- Six-step pipeline, each step idempotent for a given Sunday: plan → grocery list →
  AnyList push → recipe page build → dashboard build → commit/push → email.
- Shopping happens in the AnyList mobile app, grouped by store aisle. The dashboard
  and grocery JSON deliberately keep full recipe-accurate detail so they can be
  cross-checked against the filtered AnyList contents.
- The weekly routine has scoped authorization to auto-commit and push a specific
  list of paths. That authorization does not extend to any other edit in this repo.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- 23 recipes in `data/recipes.json`, 20 vegetarian, spanning primary proteins:
  beans, cheese, chickpeas, eggs, halloumi, lentils, meat, nuts-seeds, tempeh, tofu,
  and none. 15 have cleaned `instructions` and a published `page_url`; the rest get
  them the first time they are selected into a week.
- Vegetarian-first: at most one non-vegetarian dinner per week, and rarely. Meat
  recipes always carry a vegetarian substitution note.
- No allergies or other hard dietary constraints beyond the vegetarian-first rule.
- Ratings and exclusions in `data/preferences.json` steer selection: `never` and
  `exclusions` remove a recipe entirely; disliked recipes are down-weighted, loved
  ones boosted. Two salad-style recipes are tagged `"role": "light"` and surface less
  often as mains.
- Hosting is GitHub Pages from the public `markwyand-home/meal-planner` repo
  (`main`, `/docs`) at `https://markwyand-home.github.io/meal-planner/` — a fixed URL
  that redeploys on push. Recipes and plan data are publicly visible; no secrets live
  in the repo.
- Static HTML is generated by Python (standard library only). Node is used solely for
  the AnyList push.
- The AnyList client is unofficial and reverse-engineered; an app-side change can
  break it. When it does, the push must fail loudly — the grocery list still exists
  in `data/plans/` and on the dashboard.
- The single secret (AnyList credentials) lives at `~/.meal-planner/anylist.env`,
  deliberately outside the project folder so it never syncs to cloud storage. Its
  contents are never printed.
- Ingredient filtering for AnyList is rule-driven (`data/anylist_rules.json`): pantry
  staples are omitted, fresh-produce measurements are swapped for whole-item buys,
  and each item is assigned an AnyList aisle category. Fresh garlic is never treated
  as a staple; noodles/pasta/tofu always override exclusion rules.

## Evidence on Hand

- `Recipes/` — 19 saved source pages as `.mhtml`, the closed universe of meals.
- `data/recipes.json` — the structured corpus described above.
- `data/plans/` — real weekly plans and grocery lists from 2026-07-26 onward.
- `data/history.json` — selection history used for recency and repeat avoidance.
- `data/preferences.json` — real household feedback (one recipe rated `never` after
  the household did not enjoy it, 2026-08-23).
- `docs/index.html` and `docs/recipes/` — the currently shipped dashboard and clean
  recipe pages.

No testimonials, customers, benchmarks, pricing, or third-party validation exist for
this product, and none should ever be invented — it is a private household tool that
happens to be hosted publicly.

## Product Principles

1. **The closed corpus is sacred.** Nothing enters rotation without being explicitly
   added by the household. Never source, suggest, or silently insert an outside
   recipe.
2. **Variety is the product.** Both axes — across weeks and within a week — matter,
   and protein repetition is the failure the household most notices.
3. **A partial run is a failed run.** Report exactly what did and did not happen;
   never present a stale dashboard or unpushed list as current.
4. **Recipe-accurate detail survives the shopping filter.** What goes to AnyList is a
   convenience view; the grocery JSON and dashboard remain the full, checkable truth.
5. **Every instruction lives in the repo.** Scheduled runs have no memory, so
   decisions made in conversation are written down or they do not exist.
