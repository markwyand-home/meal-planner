A Farrotto for All Seasons

CheeseVegetarian45 minserves 4

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1 tablespoon unsalted butter
  • 1/2 small onion, chopped
  • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper to taste
  • Dash of dried red pepper flakes
  • 2 cups pearled farro
  • 1/2 cup dry white wine
  • 4 cups vegetable stock, store-bought or homemade (page 232), warmed
  • 1/3 cup grated Parmesan cheese
  • Summer add-in: 1 cup quartered cherry tomatoes
  • Summer add-in: uncooked kernels from 2 medium ears of corn
  • Summer add-in: chopped fresh basil, to finish

Instructions

  1. Combine the olive oil and 1 tablespoon of the butter in a deep medium-size skillet over medium heat. Add the onion, salt, black pepper, and red pepper flakes and cook until the onion is softened, stirring occasionally, about 3 minutes.

  2. Stir in the farro and wine and increase the heat to medium-high. Stir until most of the wine has been absorbed, then start adding vegetable stock in 1-cup drizzles, adding more as the liquid is absorbed. (You want the grains to always be just slightly submerged.) This is when you should prepare your seasonal vegetable add-in.

  3. When the farro is cooked (porridge but still a little toothy, like risotto, 30 to 35 minutes), turn off the heat and stir in the remaining tablespoon butter, the Parmesan, and the desired vegetable combination.

  4. Summer add-in: toss in 1 cup quartered cherry tomatoes and the uncooked kernels from 2 medium ears of corn. Serve the farrotto in bowls topped with chopped fresh basil.

Notes: From The Weekday Vegetarians by Jenny Rosenstrach, p.50. This is a seasonal template recipe — the book offers different vegetable add-ins by season. Defaulted here to the Summer add-in (cherry tomatoes, corn, basil) since that's what's in season now; the book's other variants (Fall: roasted honeynut squash with crispy sage; Winter: roasted Brussels sprouts and peas; Spring: sauteed mushrooms and asparagus) can be swapped in later on request. Times are estimated except the farro's stated 30-35 minute cook window.
